The Prasad Lab

Researching how AI is reshaping work, and how to make that change good for people.

We study how generative AI will reshape work, and how to make sure that change supports people's productivity and well-being along with the organizations they work for. The work grows out of long-standing interests in fairness and bias in hiring assessments, the workplace experiences of diverse groups, and careful research methods. Each of those matters more now that AI is entering the workplace.

Our projects follow three threads: how organizations can hire well with AI tools, how AI can help people feel better and get more done at work, and how AI can help us understand the experiences of many different groups of workers.

In one recent study, people who talked through their jobs with a chatbot built to help them find meaning in their work felt more authentic pride in it, and that pride predicted seeing the work as more meaningful. Other current projects ask:

  • Whether AI-generated follow-up questions in asynchronous video interviews improve measurement, and how they change the candidate's experience. Supported by SIOP.
  • Whether large language models can speed up the slow work of turning critical incidents into situational judgment tests.
  • How occupational tasks and vocational interests relate to where AI is being used at work.
  • Why women appear to adopt AI at lower rates than men, and what that gap means for their careers.

Across all of it, the aim is the same: using AI to change how we think about and experience work for the better.
